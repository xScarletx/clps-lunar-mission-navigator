How it is computed, and what is missing
  • Moon and Sun positions: the astronomy-engine library. Lunar orientation: IAU 2009 model (mean Earth / rotation axis frame). Everything in UTC, in one-hour steps.
  • Checked against PyEphem on 80 dates between 2026 and 2030: Earth’s direction agrees within 0.03° and the Sun’s within 0.3°. Not checked against NASA’s SPICE.
  • Sun: counts as visible when its upper limb clears the set horizon. Earth: when its center does. Earth is computed with parallax, as seen from the surface rather than from the Moon’s center.
  • Azimuth: 0° is local north and increases toward the east. At the poles, local north is the direction of the 0° meridian.
  • Missing: real terrain (the LOLA elevation model from the LRO orbiter). With it, each site’s horizon would be its own silhouette, and permanently shadowed regions would show up on their own.
  • The direct-to-Earth link here only requires Earth to be above the horizon. It ignores minimum antenna elevation and ground-station availability.
